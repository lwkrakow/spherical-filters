Research motivation & investigation path

Selective trust for aerial appearance tracking

An approachable guide to moving from one spherical uncertainty scale to a filter that can trust some feature directions more than others.

The hypothesis we want to test

We can know what to trust without knowing exactly what comes next.

As a camera moves around a vehicle, its visual embedding can change even though the vehicle's identity has not. We may not be able to predict that change precisely. We may still be able to learn which feature directions are likely to disagree, and by how much.

Our proposal is to tolerate those expected discrepancies, while giving more weight to appearance evidence that remains dependable.

Why investigate a different uncertainty model?

Our tracker uses cropped target images to help maintain vehicle identity alongside its position and motion estimates. A normalized image embedding is a compact description of appearance: a vector with unit length.

The current von Mises–Fisher (vMF) model describes a mean direction and one concentration value, κ. This is compact and useful. Its limitation for this investigation is that uncertainty is rotationally symmetric around the mean: it cannot independently widen tolerance along one feature direction while keeping another direction precise.

A plausible same-vehicle change

A new viewing angle changes a combination of embedding features. If that pattern is often unreliable, the mismatch should count less against an association.

Evidence that still matters

Another combination of features may remain relatively stable. A mismatch there can still provide useful evidence that this is a different vehicle.

A “feature direction” can combine many embedding coordinates. We do not assume that a particular coordinate means color, shape, or identity.

04 · INVESTIGATION

Compare directions of appearance spread with pose-related appearance patterns. Use them to build uncertainty models and evaluate tracking.

What “trust those features less” means

For association: an expected discrepancy counts less against a possible match.

For the appearance update: an unreliable observation component moves the stored estimate less. More reliable components can still contribute strongly.

This first investigation does not require a complete embedding transition law. It does require evidence that the chosen uncertainty patterns are useful on observations excluded from calibration.

The investigations, in order

  1. Find the error patterns. With one embedding backbone, observe the same vehicles across controlled viewpoint changes, crop variation, and partial occlusion. Measure which directions change and how many directions matter.
  2. Predict their reliability. Compare Within-Class Tangent PCA (WC-TPCA), which follows appearance spread, with Tangent PLS (T-PLS), which uses viewpoint labels to learn pose-related patterns. Investigate how viewpoint change and image quality affect the uncertainty.
  3. Test transfer. Hold out complete vehicle identities and sequences. Check whether calibration remains useful across viewpoints and, subsequently, real offroad imagery.
  4. Measure the tracking tradeoff. Compare tuned vMF, isotropic tangent filtering, and anisotropic tangent filtering. Measure false associations and identity switches alongside track retention.
  5. Then compare backbones and cost. Evaluate the current 128D model and selected DINO or CLIP variants. Calibrate each separately, and benchmark actual update time on the intended hardware.

The geometry applies to normalized embeddings of different dimensions. How well the uncertainty patterns separate nuisance variation from identity remains an empirical question for each backbone.

What is established, and what is our hypothesis?

Uncertainty-aware embedding comparison has precedent: Probabilistic Face Embeddings represents uncertainty in image features and uses it for matching and fusion in face recognition.

Our hypothesis: a directional uncertainty model can also improve vehicle identity association in aerial, offroad tracking. That application benefit has to be measured.
