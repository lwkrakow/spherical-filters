Research Engineering Architecture & Investigation Roadmap

Aerial Target Tracking on Riemannian Feature Manifolds

Integrating 128D visual embeddings and physics-based observation noise calibration into Multi-Hypothesis Tracking (MHT).

Note on Embedding Dimension & Backbone Agnosticism:

While the examples, dimensions, and visualizers highlighted throughout this guide use 128D (based on our current baseline, Embedding Gamma 2), this Riemannian manifold filtering framework is mathematically model-agnostic. It applies directly to higher-dimensional visual backbones such as DINO / DINOv2 (384D / 768D) and CLIP (512D). Part of our proposed engineering investigation is to evaluate which visual embedding backbone provides the cleanest separation between viewpoint-induced feature drift and invariant vehicle identity.

Executive Summary

Problem Context & Motivation

In our aerial multi-target tracking pipeline, target belief states must track not only 3D kinematics (position, velocity, heading) from cropped detector bounding boxes, but also visual identity embeddings on the unit hypersphere (y ∈ Sd-1).

Over recent months, we relied on a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution. While mathematically clean, vMF has a fundamental flaw: it is strictly isotropic. A single scalar concentration parameter κ forces uncertainty to be rotationally symmetric and identical across all 128 dimensions.

In our tracker, we already possess kinematic estimates of the target's 3D pose, UAS camera gimbal angles, and relative viewing geometry (azimuth φ, elevation θ). Forcing this rich physical knowledge through a single scalar κ wastes information:

  • Viewpoint Smear: When our UAS circles or climbs over a vehicle, viewpoint-induced appearance shifts cause the isotropic filter to either over-adapt (eroding invariant identity features) or under-adapt (triggering false track drops in MHT gating).
  • The Core Objective: We want to choose an embedding method—whether that is Embedding Gamma 2, DINO, or CLIP—and establish a reasonable, physics-informed noise calibration based on our sensor and flight models so that observation noise appropriately impacts the visual embedding state.
Architecture

The Architectural Arc: Evolution of the Filter

Phase 01 • Baseline

von Mises-Fisher (vMF)

Circular uncertainty cone governed by single scalar κ. Viewpoint-blind; cannot accommodate camera orbit drift without inflating variance across invariant identity dimensions.

Contour: Strictly Circular
Phase 02 • Viable Models

Kent Distribution (FB5)

Explored true spherical analogue to a full Gaussian, allowing elliptical contours on S2. Proved directional concept, but hit severe computational bottlenecks in 128D (hypergeometric normalizers).

Contour: Elliptical (3D only)
Phase 03 • Production Engine

Riemannian EKF (R-EKF)

Investigated scalable production implementation. Transposes filtering to Euclidean tangent space TμSd-1 with Log/Exp maps. Supports full anisotropic covariance in <0.05 ms.

Scalability: 128D / 512D / 768D
Phase 04 • Calibration

Tangent PLS Calibration

Current milestone. Uses synthetic vehicle datasets and UAS telemetry errors to construct calibrated, directional observation noise matrices Rt directly on the tangent plane.

Output: Physically Grounded Rt

Next Steps

Proposed Investigations for the Engineering Team

Before locking the tracker configuration into flight software, we are tasking the research team with the following simulation investigations using our synthetic vehicle dataset:

Investigation 1: WC-TPCA vs. T-PLS

Run both calibration routines on our synthetic car render bank. Evaluate the scree plot of eigenvalues (WC-TPCA) versus the cross-validation R2 score of pose recovery (Tangent PLS). Measure Grassmannian subspace angle to check for diagonal cross-axis mixing.

Investigation 2: Cross-Backbone Comparison

Apply the Tangent PLS calibration pipeline across our candidate models: Embedding Gamma 2 (128D), DINO / DINOv2 (384D / 768D), and CLIP (512D). Evaluate which manifold provides the cleanest separation between viewpoint drift and identity invariance.

Investigation 3: End-to-End MHT Benchmarking

Simulate a UAS executing an orbit-and-climb trajectory over a target vehicle with distractor cars. Compare track retention and false-drop rates between: (a) Baseline vMF, (b) Isotropic R-EKF, and (c) Tangent PLS Pose-Calibrated Anisotropic R-EKF.
